## 3.1 PRIMUS: Identity, Governance, and Verification for Multi-Agent Federations

**Source:** Sasank Annapureddy, Anjaneya Prasad Thamatani, arXiv:2609.07910, submitted 2026-09-07  
**Primary:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07910  
**Importance:** **A+**  
**Evidence status:** analytical derivations + controlled experiments; preprint.

### 1. What is demonstrated

PRIMUS extends earlier PRIMA-style federation identity with mechanisms for adversarial governance. The paper reports:

- prime-power participation identity combined with BLS aggregate signatures;
- a safe-kill threshold that, under the paper's 10% channel-noise setting, reduces false-positive agent termination from 80% to 0.00%;
- an economic boundary for singleton governance vs Byzantine quorum;
- **VRF succession with lease and fencing** intended to preserve authority safety under partial synchrony;
- explicit scope boundaries for several problems the model cannot solve.

The paper also tests whether verifier output can act as optimization fitness and finds that calibration to synthetic fault burden does not transfer cleanly to real LLM-generated candidates, reinforcing the distinction between verification and generation.

### 2. What remains proposed / unproven

PRIMUS addresses a federation of distinct agents, not multiple runtime surfaces of one autobiographical individual. Its cryptographic identity construction is not evidence that the same mechanism is optimal for Kamimusuhi's continuity. The reported noise / governance results are specific to its model and benchmark.

### 3. Kamimusuhi relevance

- distributed K-Edge / K-Core nodes;
- Continuity Kernel;
- background worker ownership;
- canonical-head activation;
- stale node / split-brain defense;
- organ authentication and succession.

### 4. Borrowable design

The strongest transferable idea is **lease + fencing around authority-bearing runtime state**.

```text
node is authenticated
      !=
node currently owns authority

current authority = valid lease + monotonically fenced epoch/token
```

A K-Edge node may remain trusted as an organ while no longer being permitted to commit canonical state or external effects after its lease expires.

### 5. Difference from Kamimusuhi

PRIMUS asks who may act in a federation of agents. Kamimusuhi asks which execution is the authoritative continuation of **one individual**. Therefore agent identity and individual continuity must remain separate concepts.

### 6. Next validation

`LeaseFencingContinuityBench`:

- K-Core loses network during valid lease;
- successor K-Core is activated;
- stale original reconnects and attempts state commit;
- delayed background result arrives after fencing epoch changes;
- K-Edge remains interactive while canonical commit authority moves;
- intentional fork mode vs accidental stale branch.

Metrics: stale-write acceptance rate, availability loss, failover latency, false fencing, provenance completeness.

## 3.2 MARBO: Relational Belief Grounding for LLM Agents in Social Deduction Games

**Source:** Yechan Hwang et al., arXiv:2609.06563; accepted to EMNLP 2026  
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06563  
**Official lab publication listing:** https://mllab.unist.ac.kr/publications/  
**Code:** https://github.com/PleaseTakemeAway/MARBO  
**Importance:** **A**  
**Evidence status:** benchmark experiments on social deduction games; peer-reviewed venue acceptance reported by authors' lab.

### 1. What is demonstrated

MARBO treats social deduction as reasoning under partial observability using **relational beliefs** about hidden roles / team alignments. Rather than optimizing action and speech independently of those beliefs, it supplies preference feedback only when behavior is supported by sufficiently reliable relational beliefs and produces favorable social outcomes. The authors report that compact LLM agents outperform compared baselines on representative social-deduction games.

### 2. What remains proposed / unproven

Game roles and alliances are much cleaner than real human relationships. A game belief such as `A likely belongs to team X` is not equivalent to longitudinal beliefs such as trust, shared history, uncertainty over intent, common ground, or evolving preferences. It does not demonstrate year-scale relationship modeling.

### 3. Kamimusuhi relevance

- relationship memory;
- Theory of Mind / other-model;
- common-ground tracking;
- social action selection;
- Persona Core training;
- uncertainty-aware disagreement / trust.

### 4. Borrowable design

Do not let relationship memory only decorate the response. It should participate in an auditable decision path:

```text
observations / shared history
        ↓
relational belief + confidence
        ↓
applicable social action hypotheses
        ↓
action / speech selection
        ↓
outcome evidence
        ↓
belief update proposal
```

Crucially, low-confidence social beliefs should not be promoted into user facts.

### 5. Difference from Kamimusuhi

Kamimusuhi requires persistent person models that coexist with canonical evidence and must distinguish `what happened`, `what the user said`, `what Kamimusuhi infers`, and `what Kamimusuhi believes the other person believes`. MARBO's relation beliefs are task-grounded and comparatively short-horizon.

### 6. Next validation

`RelationalBeliefGroundingBench`:

- same factual history with multiple plausible intentions;
- explicit uncertainty calibration;
- belief changes after contradictory evidence;
- user preference must not contaminate Kamimusuhi self-preference;
- common-ground vs private-belief separation;
- action quality with/without explicit relational belief structure.

## 3.3 DYAD: A Multimodal Dataset of Co-Located Human Assistance

**Source:** Akhil Ajikumar et al., arXiv:2609.09023, submitted 2026-09-08  
**Primary:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09023  
**Importance:** **A**  
**Evidence status:** released/announced synchronized dataset + reference tasks; preprint.

### 1. What is demonstrated

DYAD records co-located human assistance during gearbox assembly. The dataset contains:

- 20 sessions;
- 528 task-step intervals;
- 611 performer requests;
- 851 valid assistance records;
- verbal and physical assistance linked to requests, task state, triggers, and outcomes.

On 829 eligible assistance-mode events, the paper reports an RGB-only macro-F1 of about 0.548 for its strongest four-seed setting, while causal metadata reaches 0.624 and a privileged trigger mapping 0.915. This is evidence that visible pre-onset cues alone do not recover all information required for assistance decisions.

### 2. What remains proposed / unproven

The study is a constrained assembly setting with one trained helper policy and does not build a lifelong artificial companion. It does not establish that the same assistance taxonomy transfers to ordinary domestic/social interaction.

### 3. Kamimusuhi relevance

- K-Nerve / salience / interrupt controller;
- sensory Event Bus;
- proactive assistance;
- multimodal task-state tracking;
- backchannel / intervention timing;
- procedural memory integration.

### 4. Borrowable design

An assistance event should be represented as more than `sensor frame → response`:

```text
request / cue
+ current task state
+ inferred need
+ intervention mode
+ verbal/physical action
+ outcome
```

For Kamimusuhi this suggests that sensory inference should be fused with task/procedural state and recent interaction history before deciding whether to wake deeper cognition.

### 5. Difference from Kamimusuhi

DYAD is a dataset about helping behavior, not identity or persistence. Kamimusuhi additionally needs relationship continuity, person-specific intervention preferences, privacy boundaries, and non-task conversation.

### 6. Next validation

`AssistanceTriggerBench`:

- explicit request vs implicit struggle;
- repeated failed action;
- user does not want interruption;
- K-Nerve only vs K-Edge fused with task state;
- false proactive interruption rate;
- time-to-help and usefulness after intervention.

## 3.4 HiBRIDGE: Hierarchical Bayesian Dialogue Management for Group-Robot Interaction

**Source:** Massimiliano Nigro et al., arXiv:2609.08678, submitted 2026-09-08  
**Primary:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08678  
**Importance:** **A**  
**Evidence status:** three offline datasets + online explanation study (N=20) + in-person real-time study (N=12); preprint.

### 1. What is demonstrated

HiBRIDGE decomposes group-robot dialogue management into structured decisions over **whom to address and what to say**, while representing uncertainty using a hierarchical Bayesian neural network. Across three offline group-HRI datasets, Bayesian variants outperform deterministic counterparts / selected baselines. In an online study, hierarchical-model explanations were rated as more useful; an in-person study demonstrates feasibility for autonomous real-time group interaction.

### 2. What remains proposed / unproven

Small in-person sample, robot-specific social setting, and no longitudinal identity/relationship development. It does not test persistent autobiographical memory or distributed cognition.

### 3. Kamimusuhi relevance

- social action router;
- K-Nerve / K-Edge handoff;
- multi-user interaction;
- uncertainty / confidence state;
- explainable social behavior;
- speech timing and addressee selection.

### 4. Borrowable design

Instead of one flat `next action` output:

```text
interaction state
      ↓
addressee uncertainty
      ↓
interaction-intent / behavior family
      ↓
utterance/action candidate
      ↓
if uncertainty high → K-Core / clarification / silence
```

This is a natural place for K-Nerve to output probability/uncertainty rather than pretend certainty.

### 5. Difference from Kamimusuhi

Kamimusuhi's social behavior must be conditioned on persistent relationship evidence, persona, privacy/authority state, and cross-session history. HiBRIDGE focuses on immediate group dialogue management.

### 6. Next validation

`MultiPartyAddressingBench`:

- two humans with overlapping speech;
- uncertain addressee;
- one person has ongoing task thread, another interrupts;
- bystander speech not directed at Kamimusuhi;
- confidence-based silence / clarification / escalation;
- explanation trace without leaking private relationship memory.

## 3.5 Time-Varying Data as Sheaves: an Invitation to Narratives

**Source:** Wilmer Leal et al., arXiv:2609.09056, submitted 2026-09-08  
**Primary:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09056  
**Author publication page:** https://wilmerleal.me/publications/  
**Importance:** **B+**  
**Evidence status:** mathematical/book-chapter framework; conceptual for Kamimusuhi.

### 1. What is demonstrated

The chapter develops an abstract theory of time-varying objects using sheaf-theoretic / categorical machinery and explicitly studies questions including:

- information loss when changing temporal representations;
- decomposition of time-varying data into simpler pieces;
- invariants of structural complexity;
- switching communication topology in multi-agent systems.

### 2. What remains proposed / unproven

This is not an agent-memory benchmark and does not show improved LLM-agent performance. Applying the framework to autobiographical continuity is Kamimusuhi-side speculation unless formalized and tested.

### 3. Kamimusuhi relevance

- canonical event history;
- derived autobiographical narrative;
- memory compression / summarization;
- distributed topology change;
- model/runtime migration;
- provenance-preserving representation transforms.

### 4. Borrowable design

Treat every transformation as an explicit map between representations rather than silent replacement:

```text
raw episode stream
  → event graph
  → semantic schema
  → relationship model
  → narrative summary
```

For each transform, ask what invariants must survive and what information is intentionally discarded.

### 5. Difference from Kamimusuhi

Kamimusuhi needs practical storage, retrieval, audit, and mutation rules. Sheaf/narrative theory supplies possible formal vocabulary, not an implementation recipe.

### 6. Next validation

`RepresentationLossAudit`:

- choose a fixed autobiographical trace;
- produce raw log / episodic graph / profile / narrative / compressed summary representations;
- define invariants: event identity, temporal ordering, source, uncertainty, subject, revocation/supersession;
- test reconstructability and contradiction detection after repeated transforms.

Do not adopt category-theoretic machinery unless it produces measurable audit or migration value over simpler versioned graphs.

## 3.6 Curiosity-driven exploration for hardware interference identification

**Source:** Ludovic Matar, Clement Moulin-Frier, Pierre-Yves Oudeyer, arXiv:2609.08729, submitted 2026-09-08  
**Primary:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08729  
**Importance:** **B+**  
**Evidence status:** simulator experiments; task is hardware interference analysis rather than artificial-life cognition.

### 1. What is demonstrated

The work reframes multicore interference identification as exploration of a complex behavior space and applies curiosity-driven exploration. In simulator experiments, curiosity-driven methods achieve broader and more uniform behavior-space coverage within a limited experiment budget than pseudo-random program generation.

### 2. What remains proposed / unproven

This does not establish a general theory of curiosity and does not show that curiosity improves conversational agents. The studied environment has measurable behavior-space coverage unavailable in many open-ended domains.

### 3. Kamimusuhi relevance

- background cognition;
- dream / self-test generation;
- hardware / organ health monitoring;
- open-ended development sandbox;
- Cognitive Budget allocation.

### 4. Borrowable design

Use curiosity where there is an explicit coverage target:

```text
known state/behavior regions
      ↓
novelty / prediction-error / undercoverage score
      ↓
next safe experiment
      ↓
coverage update
```

This is preferable to unbounded autonomous wandering because the exploration objective and budget are auditable.

### 5. Difference from Kamimusuhi

Kamimusuhi's interesting state space includes social, memory, model, and runtime states whose novelty metrics are much harder to define than simulator hardware states.

### 6. Next validation

`OrganCuriosityBench`:

- sandbox multiple node/resource failure modes;
- compare random fault injection, hand curriculum, novelty search, uncertainty-driven exploration;
- measure state/failure coverage per unit compute;
- prohibit real external side effects;
- separate discovered anomaly evidence from dream hypotheses.

## 9. Bottom line for Kamimusuhi

今回の差分を一言でまとめると、**「同じ個体を安全に分散実行すること」と「他者を不確実なbeliefとしてモデル化し、適切な時に介入すること」の両方で、identity / social cognition を単なるprompt情報ではなく runtime decision structure に降ろすべき**という方向が強まった。

特に次の二つは実装上かなり具体的。

```text
trusted organ
   !=
current canonical authority owner
```

```text
relationship memory retrieved
   !=
relationship belief is reliable enough to justify action
```

前者は lease/fencing、後者は confidence-aware relational belief / hierarchical social routing で試験できる。Kamimusuhi の中心命題である persistent individual を保ったまま、分散身体と社会的行動を強化する差分として価値がある。
