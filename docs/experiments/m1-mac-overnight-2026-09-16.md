## Why CPU and not MPS (measured on this machine)

| Path | Result |
|---|---|
| `--device mps --slots 1` | works, but ~5× slower than CPU on this workload |
| `--device mps --slots ≥2` | process abort — Metal `A command encoder is already encoding to this command buffer` (PyTorch MPS thread-safety) |
| MPS + `sparse_csr` | `aten::_to_sparse_csr` not implemented for SparseMPS |
| CPU `event_csc` | fastest measured path (≈3.4 lane-sim-s/wall-s at 2k-neuron probe) |

Event-driven propagation is latency-bound edge-gather work; the dense path
that would let the GPU win is unavailable on MPS, so 12 CPU slots is the
practical optimum on this machine.

## Job-cost curve (the interesting finding)

Per-generation mean wall time per job tracked which phenotypes survived:

| Phase | gens | mean wall_s/job | what happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 0–2 | 84–99 s | founders + first mutants |
| structure explosion | 3–4 | 632–643 s | hyperactive-organ mutants (~24 min/job each) |
| purge | 5 | 112 s | catastrophic phenotypes selected out |
| converged basin | 6–11 | 87–90 s | population all-cheap again |
| second burst | 12–16 | 96–288 s | mutant tails recur periodically |
| converged | 17–20 | ~90 s | stable at stop |

Event-csc cost scales with *activity*: genomes that evolve large/hyperactive
organs simulate more edges per step, so per-job wall time is a phenotype
property, not a machine slowdown — and the same phenotypes score ~−395 and
get pruned, so the throughput curve doubles as a selection readout.

### Structure growth — "機能が追加されまくる" in numbers

Population means per generation (mutation limits: 8 organs / 512 artificial
neurons / 24 attachments):

| gen | organs | artif. neurons | attachments |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.44 | 16 | 0.96 |
| 4 | 3.04 | 94 | 6.96 |
| 8 | 5.56 | 214 | 12.6 |
| 12 | 7.73 | 296 | 19.2 |
| 16 | 7.48 | 296 | 20.4 |
| 20 | 7.63 | 312 | 21.9 |

Organisms saturate the organ cap by ~gen 11–12 and keep accruing attachments.
However: in the latest 200 evaluations only ~2 % of organ×replicate activity
readings exceed 0.01 Hz — **most accreted organs are electrically silent**
(structural drift, cheap under `resource_efficiency`), and the rare
hyperactive organs (~435 Hz, the −395-fitness phenotypes) are exactly the
jobs that took ~10–25 min and got selected out. Structure grows; function
does not yet follow — the honest headline.

### Fitness decomposition caveat

`task_score` stays ≈ −4.4 throughout (target 5 Hz vs observed ~0.57 Hz
substrate rate at 500 ms): under M1's weighting the fitness landscape is
dominated by that gap, so 21 generations show drift-driven structure growth
rather than measurable task learning. The best genome's slight edge
(−4.365 vs gen0 best −4.390) is real but small.

## Known limitations

- Synthetic substrate only — no FlyWire anatomical coordinates; the brain
  view is a schematic layout of real topology.
- Partial run: 21 of 40 planned generations completed; organ growth inflates
  per-job cost (mutant tails up to ~25 min/job), so full completion needs a
  longer window or a per-job sim-time cap. The DB/checkpoint support
  `--resume` for continuation — the run stopped at a clean generation
  boundary with a stop-checkpoint written.
- This experiment ID was itself a `--resume` of `M1-20260915-152426-54b69e`
  after a coordinator restart during setup (one `experiment_resumed_from_checkpoint`
  event); an earlier half-created attempt `M1-20260915-151915-7b7d5c` was
  discarded before any evaluations ran.
- `organ_cache` hit rate ~0: every mutated organ topology is unique, so
  topology construction repeats per job (a documented cost, not a bug).
